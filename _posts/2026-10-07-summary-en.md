## [RunwayML Launches Praxis-1, a Video-Trained Robot Control Model](https://twitter.com/lukas_m_ziegler/status/2107089881344184390) ⭐️ 7.0/10

The announcement was highlighted by @lukas_m_ziegler as the first entry in a robot news series. Robot training data is widely considered the single biggest bottleneck in robotics, since collecting real-world demonstration data is slow and expensive. By leveraging abundant video data instead, Praxis-1 could significantly accelerate progress in robot learning and open the door to more scalable foundation models for embodied AI. Praxis-1 is described as an open-weight World Action Model that turns Runway's video pre-training expertise into real-world robot control, based on the premise that robot demonstration data is limited while video data is infinite. The announcement notes modest engagement (27 likes, 4 replies), and Runway has stated it is no longer maintaining its HuggingFace organization, so model weights may be distributed elsewhere.

Video-based robot learning, sometimes called "learning by watching," lets robots learn tasks by observing human or other demonstrations on video rather than through direct teleoperation or simulation. RunwayML built its reputation on generative video tools, so applying that video expertise to robot control is a natural extension of its core technology.

## [LeCun boosts H-JEPA, a hierarchical world model learned end-to-end from pixels](https://twitter.com/ylecun/status/2107695111698002181) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Yann LeCun retweeted an announcement introducing H-JEPA, a hierarchical world model built from stacked JEPA (Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture) modules, where each level operates over a different time horizon and the whole system is learned end-to-end directly from pixels. The authors describe it as the first end-to-end learned hierarchical world model for long-horizon visual planning. World models are seen as a key path toward AI systems that understand how environments change rather than just patterns in data, and H-JEPA extends LeCun's JEPA line of work by adding hierarchy for long-horizon planning. If it holds up, this could influence how self-supervised visual planning and embodied AI research are pursued. In H-JEPA, each higher level predicts over a longer horizon and retains in its latent space only what remains predictable, while lower levels handle shorter-horizon detail; the architecture is an energy-based model that learns abstract representations that are simultaneously maximally informative and maximally predictable. The announcement came via retweets from BasileTerv987 and randall_balestr, with moderate engagement (about 25 retweets).

twitter · ylecun · Oct 7, 04:50

**Background**: JEPA (Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture) is a self-supervised learning paradigm that predicts masked latent embeddings in an abstract representation space instead of reconstructing raw pixels or generating tokens, which helps models capture high-level semantic structure. A 'world model' is an AI system that learns a compressed, predictive representation of its environment, allowing it to anticipate the consequences of actions. H-JEPA (Hierarchical JEPA) combines these ideas by stacking JEPA levels so that different layers reason over different time horizons, an approach LeCun has long advocated for autonomous intelligence.

The announcement was shared via a retweet from Chung Min Kim, highlighting its ability to train humanoids and fine-tune VLAs. QF3 addresses a key challenge in robotics: improving flow-based policies with reinforcement learning in a sample-efficient way. The method follows the critic's gradient ∇_a Q through the flow's one-step prediction, making it a direct and sample-efficient approach. Reinforcement learning (RL) is used to train robots by rewarding desired behaviors, but applying RL to flow policies efficiently remains challenging.

## [Prepending "\n\nOkay" Makes Base LLMs Match RL-Tuned Versions](https://twitter.com/berkeley_ai/status/2107702268472733865) ⭐️ 7.0/10

A tweet from Berkeley AI retweeted a finding by @sewon__min showing that simply prepending "\n\nOkay" to the response makes the Olmo base model match its RL-tuned counterpart, with the same effect observed for Qwen3-14B. This finding challenges the assumption that complex reinforcement learning fine-tuning is strictly necessary to achieve strong instruction-following behavior, and it could influence prompt engineering practices and how researchers evaluate base versus RL-tuned models. The trick is remarkably simple — just a newline-newline followed by "Okay" prepended to the response — and it was demonstrated on two open models, Olmo and Qwen3-14B, though the tweet provides no detailed methodology or explanation of why this works.

twitter · berkeley_ai · Oct 7, 05:19

**Background**: Base language models are pretrained on large text corpora but are not specifically tuned to follow instructions, while RL-tuned (reinforcement learning fine-tuned) models undergo additional training with human or AI feedback to align their outputs with user intent. Olmo is an open foundation model series from Ai2 designed for transparent NLP research, and Qwen3-14B is a 14-billion-parameter dense open-weight model from Alibaba's Qwen team released in April 2025. The finding suggests that some of the behavioral gap between base and RL-tuned models may be triggered by simple formatting cues rather than deep capability differences.

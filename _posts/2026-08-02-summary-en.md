## [ProteinGuide Officially Released for On-the-Fly Protein Design Guidance](https://twitter.com/berkeley_ai/status/2083181975381131378) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Berkeley AI announced the official release of ProteinGuide, a framework for conditioning pre-trained protein sequence generative models on user-defined properties without retraining. The tool supports models like ESM3, ProteinMPNN, and diffusion/flow matching models. ProteinGuide addresses a critical gap in protein engineering by enabling on-the-fly guidance of generative models with experimental data, potentially accelerating the design of novel proteins for therapeutics and industrial applications. It provides a principled, model-agnostic solution that could become a standard tool in computational biology. The method iteratively modulates the generation process of a pre-trained unconditional model using one or more property predictive models, avoiding the need to train a new generative model. It is applicable to masked language models (e.g., ESM3), any-order autoregressive models (e.g., ProteinMPNN), and diffusion/flow matching models (e.g., MultiFlow).

twitter · berkeley_ai · Jul 31, 13:24

**Background**: Protein sequence generative models are transforming protein engineering, but conditioning them on auxiliary information like experimental data typically requires retraining. ProteinGuide provides a general framework for 'on-the-fly' conditioning, meaning it guides the generation process of existing models without additional training. This is significant because it allows researchers to incorporate specific properties (e.g., stability, binding affinity) directly into the design process, making protein design more efficient and accessible.

## [Can AI Agents Conduct Open-Ended AI Research?](https://twitter.com/ylecun/status/2083299235911364754) ⭐️ 6.0/10

A tweet by Yann LeCun highlights a recent paper questioning whether AI agents can conduct open-ended AI research, noting that current evaluations focus on narrow, verifiable tasks. The paper proposes a new evaluation paradigm called 'shadow evaluations' to address this gap. This discussion is significant because it addresses a critical limitation in AI agent evaluation: the inability to measure performance on open-ended research tasks, which are essential for advancing AI research itself. The proposed shadow evaluation method could provide a more accurate assessment of AI agents' research capabilities, potentially influencing future AI development and deployment. The paper (arXiv:2607.27191) describes shadow evaluations, where unpublished paper authors grade AI agents' attempts to answer their research questions. This approach provides open-ended tasks, uncontaminated questions, and expert reviewers, complementing existing narrow-task evaluations and blind peer review.

twitter · ylecun · Jul 31, 21:10

**Background**: AI agents are increasingly used to automate tasks, but evaluating their ability to conduct open-ended research is challenging. Current methods either test agents on narrow, verifiable tasks, which exclude open-ended research, or submit AI-generated papers to blind peer review, which is overstretched and suffers from poor review quality. The shadow evaluation paradigm aims to bridge this gap by using expert reviewers to assess AI agents on real research questions.

Yann LeCun retweeted Minghao Fu's announcement of LeWAM, a decoder-free JEPA-based world action model that jointly imagines future outcomes and the actions to take next, claiming a 32.3× speed improvement for real-world applications. The accompanying paper appeared on arXiv and inference code plus model weights were released on GitHub, with a pretrained robotics checkpoint (lewam-so101-pretrained) also posted on Hugging Face. A 32.3× speedup could make such world models practical for real-time control, and LeCun's endorsement signals that JEPA-style latent prediction remains a central pillar of his long-term vision for AI beyond token-generating LLMs. LeWAM is described as a decoder-free JEPA world action model whose latent space is claimed to be more informative than that of LeWM, and it uses diffusion-steering-based techniques. The speedup figure of 32.3× is a self-reported claim from the authors, and the tweet itself provides only a brief teaser without technical detail or independent verification.

World models are AI systems that learn an internal simulation of how an environment evolves, letting an agent 'imagine' the consequences of actions before taking them — a capability seen as key to robotics and embodied AI. LeWAM extends this idea by jointly modeling future states and the actions that produce them, building on earlier JEPA-based world models such as LeWM.

## [Stanford AI Lab Introduces Mosaic for Diffusion Language Models](https://twitter.com/StanfordAILab/status/2108606994668212232) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Stanford AI Lab announced Mosaic, a constrained decoding framework designed specifically for diffusion language models (DLMs), which reportedly significantly improves their performance. The work was shared by researcher @meihuadang and endorsed by advisor Stefano Ermon, who described it as 'constrained decoding for diffusion LLMs done right,' framing finite automata as the core mechanism. Constrained decoding is already the default for structured LLM-to-program interfaces, but applying it to diffusion language models has been an open problem because DLMs generate tokens non-sequentially. Mosaic could make DLMs viable for structured generation tasks like JSON, code, and formal grammars, potentially strengthening a class of models seen as an alternative to autoregressive LLMs. The framework views finite automata as the constraint representation, which is the standard approach for guaranteeing that generated output belongs to a specified formal language. The announcement provides limited technical detail, and the tweet itself notes only that Mosaic 'significantly improves DLMs' performance' without publishing benchmark numbers in the snippet.

twitter · StanfordAILab · Oct 9, 17:14

**Background**: Diffusion language models generate text by iteratively denoising a sequence rather than predicting tokens left-to-right like autoregressive models such as GPT, and they are widely believed to offer advantages in parallelism and controllability. Constrained decoding forces a model's output to conform to a grammar, regex, or schema by filtering the vocabulary at each generation step, typically by compiling the constraint into a state machine and only allowing tokens that can still reach an accepting state. Mosaic's contribution is adapting this automata-based masking approach to the non-sequential generation process of DLMs.

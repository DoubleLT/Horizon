## [ETH Zürich robotic hand walks on its own fingers](https://twitter.com/lukas_m_ziegler/status/2100156077291557210) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Researchers at ETH Zürich have developed a robotic hand that can walk across surfaces using its own fingers, with no wheels, legs, or separate drive mechanism attached underneath. The same fingers used for grasping objects also serve as the means of locomotion, allowing the hand to carry itself from place to place. This demonstrates that grasping and locomotion can be unified in a single mechanism, which could lead to more versatile and compact robotic end-effectors that can reposition themselves without a separate mobile base. It matters for robotics researchers exploring bio-inspired designs and for applications where a manipulator must move around independently. The key technical novelty is that no separate drive mechanism is used for locomotion — the fingers themselves provide both grasping and walking, similar to the disembodied hand 'Thing' from The Addams Family. The demo comes from ETH Zürich's Soft Robotics Lab, which has previously produced tendon-driven anthropomorphic hands such as the ORCA hand with 17 degrees of freedom.

twitter · lukas_m_ziegler · Sep 16, 09:33

**Background**: Robotic hands are typically designed for manipulation — grasping and holding objects — while mobility is handled by a separate robot arm or mobile base. Tendon-driven anthropomorphic hands mimic human hand anatomy using cables and actuators to move joints, and bio-inspired robotics seeks to replicate natural structures to gain dexterity and efficiency. This project blurs the line between manipulation and locomotion by making one set of fingers do both jobs.

## [Reach Robotics Demonstrates Electric Underwater Robot Arm Turning a Valve](https://twitter.com/lukas_m_ziegler/status/2100153772362039487) ⭐️ 6.0/10

Reach Robotics demonstrated an underwater robot arm successfully turning a valve, showcasing its electric actuation technology for ROV-mounted manipulators. The demonstration highlights an alternative to the traditional hydraulic systems used in subsea arms. Electric actuation could eliminate the heavy oil power packs and large work-class ROVs required by hydraulic subsea manipulators, enabling smaller, more portable ROVs to perform intervention tasks previously reserved for human divers or large vehicles. This shift could lower costs and expand access to subsea inspection and intervention across industries like offshore energy and defense. Reach Robotics offers manipulators such as the Reach Alpha and Reach Bravo, with the Reach Alpha rated to 300m depth, and the Reach X designed as a power-dense, compact manipulator for portable ROVs. twitter · lukas_m_ziegler · Sep 16, 09:24

**Background**: Subsea manipulators are robotic arms mounted on remotely operated vehicles (ROVs) to perform tasks like turning valves, cutting, and sampling underwater. Traditionally these arms are hydraulic, requiring an oil power pack, significant mass, and a work-class ROV large enough to carry the system. Electric actuation replaces hydraulic fluid with electromechanical motors and drives, offering potential benefits in weight, efficiency, and control precision.

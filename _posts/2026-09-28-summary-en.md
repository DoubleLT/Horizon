## [Spider-bot welder's control system faces kilovolt arc transients](https://twitter.com/lukas_m_ziegler/status/2104145738070384966) ⭐️ 6.0/10

Lukas M. Ziegler published a Twitter thread analyzing the control system of a spider-bot welder from Diden Robotics, noting that every signal in the system is read against ground. He highlighted that when a welding arc strikes and breaks, it can momentarily generate several kilovolts that couple through the air into the control electronics. This highlights a critical engineering challenge for mobile welding robots: arc strike transients can corrupt or damage ground-referenced control signals, so robust isolation and grounding are essential for reliable autonomous welding in shipyards and other industrial settings. Solving this affects the viability of deploying legged robots in high-energy industrial tasks. The arc strike/break transient can reach several kilovolts and couples capacitively through the air, meaning even signals not directly connected to the welding circuit can be affected. This makes proper grounding, shielding, and isolation design non-trivial for a compact legged robot carrying its own welding equipment.

twitter · lukas_m_ziegler · Sep 27, 09:46

**Background**: Diden Robotics develops the Diden Spider, a legged robot that uses electro-permanent magnet (EPM) technology to move across vertical surfaces and ceilings, performing welding, inspection, and maintenance in hard-to-reach environments. It has reportedly been deployed with major Korean shipbuilders. In arc welding, the sudden establishment and interruption of the arc produces fast, high-voltage transients that can couple into nearby electronics, a well-known problem in industrial control systems.

## [Microsoft Open-Sources SkillOpt for Agent Learning](https://twitter.com/RodmanAi/status/2060603132124750283) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Microsoft open-sourced SkillOpt, a system that improves AI agents by training a markdown file instead of retraining the underlying model. The optimizer edits a natural-language skill document using trajectory-driven edits and validation-gated updates. This approach dramatically reduces the cost and complexity of improving AI agents, as it avoids retraining large models with billions of parameters. It enables faster iteration and easier deployment of agent skills, potentially accelerating the adoption of AI agents in production. SkillOpt treats a compact markdown file as the trainable state of a frozen language agent, using a separate optimizer model to make bounded edits. An edit is accepted only if it strictly improves a held-out validation score, ensuring reliable skill improvement.

twitter · RodmanAi · May 30, 06:04

**Background**: Traditional AI agent improvement requires fine-tuning the underlying large language model, which is computationally expensive and risky. SkillOpt instead optimizes a natural-language skill document that the agent reads at inference time, making the process lightweight and interpretable. This is part of a growing trend of using markdown files to define and coordinate AI agent behavior.

## [80g Rat Exoskeleton for Neurorehabilitation Research](https://twitter.com/lukas_m_ziegler/status/2061127999945073030) ⭐️ 6.0/10

Researchers from the University of Tsukuba and Nagoya University have developed the first rodent exoskeleton that supports an entire rat hindlimb, weighing only 80 grams (about 1/4 of a rat's body weight) and using Bowden cables for actuation. This lightweight exoskeleton enables new possibilities for studying neurorehabilitation in rodent models, potentially accelerating the development of therapies for human spinal cord injury and stroke recovery. The exoskeleton uses Bowden cables, which are flexible cables that transmit force through an inner wire sliding within an outer housing, allowing remote actuation without adding weight to the limb. The device weighs only 80 grams, minimizing interference with natural movement.

**Background**: Rodent models are widely used in neuroscience research to study motor control and rehabilitation after neurological injury. Exoskeletons for humans are common, but scaling them down to rodents is challenging due to weight and size constraints. Bowden cables are commonly used in bicycle brakes and prosthetic devices for their flexibility and low friction.

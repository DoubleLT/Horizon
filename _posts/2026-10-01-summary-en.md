Researchers at Duke University's General Robotics Lab have unveiled MARBLE, a fully sealed spherical robot that moves by shifting three internal mass sliders along mutually orthogonal axes. This internal mass redistribution lets it roll omnidirectionally on land and propel itself on water without any external moving parts. Because MARBLE has no exposed actuators, propellers, or legs, it is inherently robust to water, debris, vegetation, and physical contact, making it a promising platform for field robots that must traverse varied terrain and obstacles. Its amphibious capability also addresses a long-standing challenge in robotics: achieving efficient propulsion in both aquatic and terrestrial environments with a single mechanism. The robot uses three mass sliders mounted on mutually orthogonal axes inside a sealed sphere, and its motion is driven entirely by internal mass redistribution. While this design avoids external moving parts, controlling a spherical robot through internal mass shifting is nonlinear and mechanically challenging, requiring careful balancing and weight distribution.

twitter · lukas_m_ziegler · Sep 30, 11:51

**Background**: Spherical robots are a class of mobile robots where the body itself is a ball, often driven by internal mechanisms such as pendulums, sliders, or fluid-filled pipes. Traditional amphibious robots typically rely on separate propulsion systems for land and water, such as legs plus propellers, which adds complexity and external parts that can be damaged. MARBLE belongs to a lineage of internal-actuation spherical robots, but its fully sealed, omnidirectional amphibious design distinguishes it from earlier ground-only or multi-modal counterparts.

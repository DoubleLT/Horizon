Researchers at Stanford, including Dorsa Sadigh and Megha Byte, have proposed Proximal State Nudging (PSN), a shared autonomy algorithm that estimates the learnability of states to nudge humans toward actions that improve skill development while maintaining task performance. This work addresses the critical issue of skill atrophy in human-AI shared control systems, such as semi-autonomous driving or racing, where over-reliance on AI can degrade human skills. PSN could enhance long-term human competence and safety in such systems. PSN is inspired by the Zone of Proximal Development theory from cognitive psychology and maintains an estimator φzpd,t(s) to assess each state's learnability. The algorithm jointly optimizes for skill development and task performance in rapid-control domains like high-performance racing.

twitter · StanfordAILab · Jul 13, 01:31

**Background**: Skill atrophy refers to the gradual decline of human capability when AI assistance is heavily relied upon, posing safety risks in shared-control systems. Proximal State Nudging uses local, data-informed adjustments to balance immediate performance with long-term learning, drawing from educational psychology concepts.

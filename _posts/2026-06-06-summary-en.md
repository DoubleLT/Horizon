Researchers at CVPR presented a new method where visual language models (VLMs) reason through a latent bottleneck in the visual domain, significantly improving their ability to discover visual concepts. This approach addresses a key limitation of VLMs that project visual information into language space only once, potentially leading to more robust and interpretable multimodal reasoning across applications like robotics and image understanding. The latent bottleneck forces the model to compress necessary visual information into a compact representation before reasoning, preventing shortcuts and encouraging deeper visual reasoning.

twitter · berkeley_ai · Jun 6, 01:05

**Background**: Vision-language models (VLMs) typically encode visual inputs into language tokens and then reason purely in text space. This can lose fine-grained visual details. The latent bottleneck technique introduces an intermediate visual reasoning step, compelling the model to rely on compressed visual features before generating text.

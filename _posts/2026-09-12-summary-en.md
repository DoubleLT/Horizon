## [DeepMind uses pose control models to reconstruct unfilmed memories for documentary](https://twitter.com/GoogleDeepMind/status/2098417966647808219) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Google DeepMind announced that its team paired restored archival photos with pose control models to capture the mannerisms and micro-expressions of Burt and Ethelle, bringing the day they first met to life for the new documentary 'Love, Rendered.' The announcement was shared via the @GoogleDeepMind Twitter account, highlighting a novel application of generative AI to historical memory reconstruction. This demonstrates how generative AI can move beyond simple image restoration to reconstruct dynamic human presence from static historical artifacts, potentially transforming documentary filmmaking and how we preserve personal and cultural memory. It signals a growing trend of AI being used not just for efficiency but for creative and emotional storytelling. The approach combines AI image restoration of archival photos with pose control models, which typically use skeletal keypoints (like OpenPose) to guide the posture and movement of generated subjects. Micro-expressions — brief, involuntary facial expressions lasting less than half a second — were specifically targeted to add emotional authenticity to the reconstructed scenes.

twitter · GoogleDeepMind · Sep 11, 14:26

**Background**: Pose control models are a technique used with text-to-image diffusion models like Stable Diffusion, where skeletal pose data (such as OpenPose) conditions the generation to maintain a specific human posture. Image restoration by AI involves taking damaged, faded, or noisy historical photos and estimating a clean, original version. Micro-expressions are very brief facial movements that reveal genuine emotion, often lasting less than half a second, and are difficult to consciously control. Combining these technologies allows filmmakers to animate historical figures from still photographs in a way that feels emotionally authentic.

## [MicroAGI's MicroSLAM Tops LaMAria Egocentric SLAM Benchmark](https://twitter.com/lukas_m_ziegler/status/2098052912203129198) ⭐️ 7.0/10

MicroAGI announced MicroSLAM, a monocular SLAM system for human egocentric video built by its Zürich-based computer vision team in collaboration with ETH Zürich. The company reports that MicroSLAM ranks first on the LaMAria benchmark for monocular SLAM and second against any method, including multi-sensor systems. If head-mounted camera footage can be reliably localized and mapped, everyday human video becomes a scalable source of robot training data, potentially reducing reliance on expensive teleoperation and motion-capture rigs. This matters for robot learning, AR/VR, and wearable computing, where egocentric perception is central. MicroSLAM is monocular, meaning it uses a single camera stream rather than stereo or LiDAR, which keeps hardware simple but makes scale estimation and robustness harder. The LaMAria benchmark spans roughly 22 hours and 70 km of city-scale egocentric trajectories with centimeter-accurate surveyed ground truth, and the announcement has already inspired at least one independent browser-based reimplementation.

twitter · lukas_m_ziegler · Sep 10, 14:16

**Background**: SLAM (Simultaneous Localization and Mapping) lets a device build a map of an unknown environment while tracking its own position within it, and is fundamental to robots, drones, and AR glasses. Monocular SLAM uses only one camera, which is cheap and lightweight but suffers from scale ambiguity and drift. LaMAria is a 2025 ICCV benchmark from ETH Zürich that evaluates visual-inertial SLAM on egocentric, city-scale data captured with glasses-like devices such as Project Aria.

This experiment adds to growing evidence that multi-agent AI ensembles can produce collective behaviors that no single model was explicitly programmed to exhibit, which matters for both AI safety evaluation and the design of future agentic systems. As multi-agent deployments become more common, understanding how small subgroups can steer the whole population is increasingly important. The experiment used 100 agents solving math problems, with emergent convergence triggered when roughly 9% of agents adopted similar behavior. twitter · GoogleDeepMind · Sep 10, 11:03

Math problem solving is a common benchmark because it offers objectively verifiable answers.

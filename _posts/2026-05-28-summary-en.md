## [Google DeepMind Unveils Gemini Embedding 2, a Native Multimodal Model](https://twitter.com/GoogleDeepMind/status/2059561362313925046) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Google DeepMind has announced Gemini Embedding 2, a native multimodal embedding model that can map text, images, video, audio, and documents into a single unified embedding space. The model is built on the Gemini architecture and is now available via Google Cloud and the DeepMind model library. This is a significant advancement because it enables seamless cross-modal search, retrieval, and understanding without needing separate models for each modality. It could dramatically simplify and accelerate AI applications in areas like multimedia search, content moderation, and multimodal RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation). Gemini Embedding 2 generates 3072-dimensional vectors and accepts interleaved multimodal inputs, including combinations of text, images, audio, and video. The model reportedly reduces latency by up to 70% compared to previous approaches that required separate LLM inference, and nearly doubles semantic similarity scores for text-image and text-video pairs.

twitter · GoogleDeepMind · May 27, 09:04

**Background**: Embedding models convert data (like text or images) into numerical vectors that capture semantic meaning, enabling similarity search and clustering. Traditional embedding models are often unimodal (e.g., only text or only images), requiring separate models for each data type. A native multimodal embedding model like Gemini Embedding 2 processes multiple modalities in a single model, learning a shared representation space from the ground up.

## [mKernel: Fast Multi-Node Multi-GPU Fused Kernels Released](https://twitter.com/berkeley_ai/status/2059411345905459410) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The UC Compute Lab released mKernel, a collection of fast multi-node, multi-GPU fused kernels that combine intra-node NVLink communication, inter-node RDMA, and compute into a single persistent kernel. mKernel addresses a critical bottleneck in distributed deep learning by reducing communication overhead and improving GPU utilization, which can significantly accelerate training of large models across multiple nodes. The kernels leverage NVIDIA's Tensor Memory Accelerator (TMA) introduced in Hopper architecture, achieving up to 74% of theoretical peak bandwidth on B200 GPUs with full compute utilization.

twitter · berkeley_ai · May 26, 23:08

**Background**: In deep learning, a fused kernel combines multiple operations (e.g., matrix multiply, activation) into a single GPU program to reduce memory access and overhead. Distributed training across multiple GPUs and nodes typically requires explicit communication between devices, which can become a performance bottleneck. mKernel fuses communication and computation into one kernel, minimizing data movement and synchronization delays.

## [Medical LLM Benchmarks Need Construct Validity](https://twitter.com/berkeley_ai/status/2059410412999991764) ⭐️ 7.0/10

A position paper argues that medical LLM benchmarks should prioritize construct validity, meaning they must measure actual clinical capabilities rather than superficial performance. The paper applies psychometric tools to evaluate existing benchmarks and finds that top-scoring models often fail on real patient records. This matters because current medical LLM benchmarks may overstate model capabilities, leading to unsafe deployment in healthcare. Prioritizing construct validity ensures benchmarks reflect real-world clinical tasks, improving trust and safety in AI-assisted medicine. The paper draws an analogy between LLM benchmarks and psychological tests, borrowing the concept of construct validity from psychometrics. Empirical evaluation showed that even models with top scores on popular benchmarks often did not perform well when working with real patient records.

twitter · berkeley_ai · May 26, 23:04

**Background**: Construct validity refers to whether a test measures the underlying concept it claims to measure. In medical AI, benchmarks like MedQA or PubMedQA are used to evaluate LLMs, but they may not capture the complexity of real clinical decision-making. This paper argues for empirical validation of benchmarks using real-world clinical data.

Researchers introduced T-Rex, a tactile-reactive dexterous manipulation framework that combines large-scale human egocentric pre-training with tactile-grounded robot mid-training, achieving a 30% absolute success rate improvement over baselines on 12 real-world tasks. This work bridges the gap between vision-based and tactile-based manipulation, enabling robots to perform force-sensitive and deformation-aware tasks that were previously challenging, advancing the frontier of dexterous robotics. T-Rex uses a Mixture-of-Transformer-Experts (MoT) architecture integrating low-frequency visuomotor planning with high-frequency tactile refinement, and was validated on a bimanual platform with 22-DoF hands and high-resolution fingertip tactile sensors.

twitter · berkeley_ai · Jun 19, 20:30

**Background**: Traditional robot manipulation relies heavily on vision, but human dexterity depends on tactile feedback. T-Rex addresses this by collecting a 100-hour tactile-rich dataset using a data-efficient recipe that prioritizes elementary motor primitives, enabling real-time tactile-reactive control.

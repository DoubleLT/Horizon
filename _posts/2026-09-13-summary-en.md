The Legged Robotics Lab at ETH Zürich demonstrated a humanoid robot jumping up to a monkey bar structure, swinging across it hand-over-hand, and dropping down to a controlled landing. The team highlighted that conventional heightfield terrain representations are unsuitable for horizontal bars, since a top-down grid of heights cannot capture thin overhanging geometry. This demonstration pushes humanoid locomotion beyond walking on mapped ground toward agile whole-body traversal of sparse 3D structures, a capability relevant to inspection, maintenance, and rescue scenarios where ladders, scaffolding, or pipes must be crossed. It also signals that perception and control pipelines built around ground-plane assumptions need rethinking for such tasks. The robot performs a full sequence — jump-up, dynamic brachiation, and landing — rather than a static hang, implying real-time control of momentum and grip timing. The key technical caveat raised is representational: heightfields are a 2.5D simplification that works well for floors and terrain but fails for thin, overhanging horizontal bars.

twitter · lukas_m_ziegler · Sep 12, 11:57

**Background**: A heightfield is a 2.5D scene representation that stores a single height value per cell in a top-down 2D grid; it is popular in robotics and AR because it is fast and memory-efficient, and it suits indoor environments where geometry varies horizontally but stays simple vertically. ETH Zürich's Legged Robotics Lab is known for legged machines such as the quadruped ANYmal, which has demonstrated agile tasks like playing badminton using reinforcement-learning controllers. Monkey-bar traversal is a common benchmark for testing whether a robot can perceive thin, overhanging structures and execute accurate whole-body motions.

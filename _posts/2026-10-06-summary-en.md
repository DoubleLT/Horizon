## [Stanford AI Lab Highlights Acto Method to Improve RL Critic for LLMs](https://twitter.com/StanfordAILab/status/2106998148413681804) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Stanford AI Lab shared a new method called Acto that improves the critic in reinforcement learning for large language models, allowing training to skip completing every rollout. The tweet, retweeted from researcher wen_kaiyue, argues that a better critic can be trusted to avoid generating full rollouts, potentially increasing RL training efficiency. Rollout generation is a major computational bottleneck in reinforcement learning for LLMs, so methods that reduce the need to complete every rollout could significantly cut training costs and speed up iteration on reasoning-focused models. This matters for researchers and labs training LLMs with RL, where efficiency gains directly translate into faster experimentation and lower compute budgets. The method centers on strengthening the critic—the component that estimates value or advantage—so it can reliably guide training without requiring full rollouts. The tweet is brief and does not provide technical specifics such as benchmarks, model sizes, or quantitative efficiency gains, so those details remain to be confirmed from the full paper.

twitter · StanfordAILab · Oct 5, 06:41

**Background**: Reinforcement learning for LLMs typically involves generating multiple candidate outputs, called rollouts, and using reward signals to update the model. Actor-critic methods use a critic to estimate how good a state or action is, which helps reduce the variance of training updates. In LLM RL, generating full rollouts is expensive because each one requires running the model through many tokens, so improving the critic to avoid unnecessary rollouts is a natural efficiency target.

Yann LeCun retweeted a link to a paper titled 'Hamiltonian JEPA: Action-Conditioned World Models with an Inherited Control State', authored by Tamim Zoabi and one other researcher (arXiv:2609.33497). The tweet itself contains only the title and a link, with no additional commentary from LeCun. LeCun's amplification signals that combining Hamiltonian mechanics with JEPA-style world models is a direction he considers promising for embodied AI, potentially influencing how researchers design action-conditioned predictive models. It could steer more attention toward physics-informed latent-space prediction as an alternative to generative reconstruction-based approaches. The paper proposes an action-conditioned world model that inherits a control state, drawing on Hamiltonian mechanics to structure the latent dynamics. Related work such as 'Beyond Isotropy in JEPAs: Hamiltonian Geometry and Symplectic...' (arXiv:2605.20107) suggests this is part of a broader effort to give JEPA representations a Hamiltonian geometric structure.

twitter · ylecun · Oct 4, 12:43

**Background**: JEPA (Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture) is a self-supervised learning framework championed by Yann LeCun that predicts embeddings in latent space rather than reconstructing raw inputs, and has been applied to images (I-JEPA) and time series. Hamiltonian mechanics is a reformulation of classical physics describing system evolution in terms of energy and phase space, and has inspired physics-informed neural networks such as Hamiltonian Neural Networks. Combining the two aims to give learned world models physically grounded, energy-conserving dynamics for planning and control in embodied agents.

## [ETH Zürich robotic hand walks on its own fingers](https://twitter.com/lukas_m_ziegler/status/2100156077291557210) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Researchers at ETH Zürich's Soft Robotics Lab have developed a robotic hand that can walk on its own fingers, using the same fingers for both grasping and locomotion without wheels or separate drive mechanisms. The project, called "Fingers as Legs," uses an off-the-shelf anthropomorphic hand weighing 818 grams with onboard Raspberry Pi, IMU, and battery, and was detailed in arXiv paper 2609.17172. This work demonstrates that a single anthropomorphic hand can achieve self-supported locomotion and manipulation without adding dedicated legs or wheels, potentially enabling more compact and versatile robots. It could influence future designs in mobile manipulation, prosthetics, and bio-inspired robotics where hardware simplicity and multi-functionality are valued. The hand is an off-the-shelf model with onboard power and computation, making it self-contained. A reinforcement learning approach accounts for the hand's unequal fingers, and the system retains the original finger design and position controller while learning to walk and manipulate.

twitter · lukas_m_ziegler · Sep 16, 09:33

**Background**: Robotic hands are typically designed for grasping and manipulation, while locomotion is handled by separate legs or wheels. Anthropomorphic hands mimic human hand structure, and making them walk requires using fingers as legs, which is challenging due to their unequal lengths and the need to support the body's weight. Reinforcement learning is a machine learning technique where an agent learns by interacting with the environment to maximize rewards, often used for robotic control.

## [MIT's Loop Closure Grasping Overcomes Versatility-Strength Tradeoff](https://twitter.com/lukas_m_ziegler/status/2093300864517619818) ⭐️ 7.0/10

MIT researchers introduced 'loop closure grasping,' a new robotic grasping concept that uses topological transformations between open-loop and closed-loop morphologies to achieve both versatile grasping and strong, gentle holding. This method was detailed in a recent paper (arXiv:2505.10552) and demonstrated with a vine-inspired gripper developed with Stanford. This breakthrough addresses a long-standing tradeoff in robotics, potentially enabling robots to handle both heavy and fragile objects with a single mechanism. It could significantly advance applications in manufacturing, logistics, and assistive robotics, where adaptable yet safe grasping is critical. The concept leverages topological transformations, allowing the gripper to switch between an open loop for positioning and a closed loop for secure holding. The MIT-Stanford collaboration demonstrated this with a vine-inspired soft gripper that can wrap, lock, and lift objects like a soft sling rather than a rigid clamp.

twitter · lukas_m_ziegler · Aug 28, 11:33

**Background**: Traditional robotic grippers often face a tradeoff: they can either form versatile grasps or provide strong and gentle holding, but rarely both. Loop closure grasping addresses this by changing the gripper's topology, transitioning from an open-loop configuration for positioning to a closed-loop configuration for stable, compliant holding. This approach is inspired by natural mechanisms like vines and slings, which can adapt to various objects while providing secure support.

## [SIGReg Pretraining Method for Video Foundation Models Announced](https://twitter.com/ylecun/status/2093550223163306036) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Yann LeCun retweeted an announcement of SIGReg, a new pretraining method for video foundation models, as part of the LeVJEPA project. The method uses a simple loss with SIGReg regularization to achieve stable training without collapse. This method could reduce the computational cost and complexity of pretraining video foundation models, making them more accessible. It also advances non-contrastive self-supervised learning, which is a significant trend in AI research. LeVJEPA is the first video encoder trained under LeJEPA's collapse-free objective, using a single encoder with an invariance loss over global and local views, regularized by SIGReg. It matches V-JEPA 2 performance with 5.6-20.8x less pretraining compute, and provides block-causal frame representations at no accuracy cost.

twitter · ylecun · Aug 29, 04:04

**Background**: Video foundation models are large neural networks pretrained on video data to learn general representations. Traditional contrastive methods require negative pairs, which are computationally expensive; non-contrastive methods like LeVJEPA avoid this, but risk representation collapse. SIGReg is a regularization technique that provably prevents collapse, enabling stable training with a simple loss.

## [LeVJEPA: New Pareto Frontier in Video Pretraining](https://twitter.com/ylecun/status/2093549515831701539) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Yann LeCun retweeted Lukas Kuhn's announcement of LeVJEPA, a new video pretraining method that achieves a new Pareto frontier in video pretraining. LeVJEPA is the first video encoder trained under the collapse-free objective of LeJEPA, requiring no hand-crafted heuristics. This is significant because LeVJEPA matches the performance of V-JEPA 2 while using 5.6-20.8x less pretraining compute, potentially making advanced video pretraining more accessible and efficient. It could influence future research in self-supervised learning and video understanding. LeVJEPA uses a single encoder trained with an invariance loss over global and local views of a clip, regularized by SIGReg, which excludes collapse with a provable guarantee. It achieves block-causal frame representations at no accuracy cost.

twitter · ylecun · Aug 29, 04:01

**Background**: Video pretraining typically relies on hand-crafted heuristics to avoid representation collapse, which can be unstable and inefficient. LeVJEPA builds on the LeJEPA objective, which is collapse-free, eliminating the need for such heuristics. The Pareto frontier refers to a trade-off where no improvement in one metric (e.g., accuracy) can be made without worsening another (e.g., compute).

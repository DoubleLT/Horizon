## [VertiGo robot drives up walls using tiltable propellers](https://twitter.com/lukas_m_ziegler/status/2101244934028234809) ⭐️ 7.0/10

A tweet by @lukas_m_ziegler highlights VertiGo, a wall-climbing robot developed by Disney Research Zurich in collaboration with ETH Zürich, which uses two tiltable propellers instead of suction cups, magnets, or dry adhesives to drive straight up vertical surfaces. The robot can transition from the ground to a wall and traverse obstacles, with the rear propeller pressing the wheels toward the wall and the front propeller providing upward thrust. This approach sidesteps the surface-compatibility limitations of conventional adhesion-based wall-climbing robots, potentially enabling inspection, rescue, or entertainment robots to operate on a much wider range of vertical surfaces. The high engagement on social media (974 likes, 118 retweets) indicates strong community interest in alternative locomotion strategies for robotics. VertiGo uses a central carbon fibre baseplate to minimize weight and maximize the thrust-to-weight ratio, with four wheels (one pair steerable) and two propellers that each have two degrees of freedom for adjusting thrust direction. The design was created during a one-year project and was published by Disney Research in December 2015.

twitter · lukas_m_ziegler · Sep 19, 09:40

**Background**: Wall-climbing robots typically rely on suction cups, magnets, or dry adhesives to stay attached to surfaces, but these methods require specific surface properties and struggle with transitions from floor to wall. VertiGo instead uses propeller thrust to press its wheels against the wall, similar to how a drone generates lift, allowing it to drive on both floors and walls. The project was a collaboration between Disney Research Zurich and ETH Zürich, first presented in 2015.

## [Stanford AI Lab Introduces CSBP for Faster Diffusion LLM Training](https://twitter.com/StanfordAILab/status/2101020335000998089) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Stanford AI Lab announced Context-Sharded Block Parallelism (CSBP), a new distributed parallelism strategy for training diffusion LLMs. It achieves up to 7.59× faster training of the DFlash2 speculative decoding drafter and 1.61× faster training in another setting, with gains that grow as context length increases. Training diffusion LLMs at long context is computationally expensive, and CSBP's context-length-scaling speedups could make such training far more practical. This matters for researchers and engineers working on diffusion-based language models and speculative decoding, where drafter training is often a bottleneck. CSBP extends block parallelism by also sharding the shared clean sequence across ranks, which is what enables scaling to long contexts. The reported 7.59× and 1.61× speedups are specific to DFlash2 drafter training and one other workload, so gains may vary by model and setup.

twitter · StanfordAILab · Sep 18, 18:47

**Background**: Diffusion LLMs generate text through an iterative denoising process rather than strictly left-to-right decoding, and training them on long sequences requires distributing computation across many devices. Block parallelism splits a sequence into blocks processed on different ranks, but the shared clean sequence becomes a bottleneck at long contexts. Speculative decoding speeds up inference by having a small drafter model propose tokens that a larger model verifies, and training that drafter efficiently is important for deployment.

## [Berkeley AI Releases ABC-130K, Largest Open Teleoperation Dataset](https://twitter.com/berkeley_ai/status/2101094946380136927) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Berkeley AI announced ABC-130K, described as the largest open teleoperation dataset to date, containing 3,500 hours and over 130,000 episodes, and the work has been accepted to CoRL 2026. Large-scale open datasets are a bottleneck for robot learning, so a dataset of this size could accelerate behavior-cloning and embodied-AI research by giving academic and industry labs a shared, reproducible foundation instead of each team collecting its own proprietary data. According to the project's own materials, ABC-130K comprises roughly 134,806 episodes across 195 tasks totaling about 3,553 hours of bimanual manipulation, with about 43,090 episodes carrying subtask annotations, collected on bimanual YAM stations with two 6-DoF arms.

twitter · berkeley_ai · Sep 18, 23:44

**Background**: Teleoperation means a human operator remotely controls a robot in real time, and the resulting trajectories of joint states, gripper actions, and video are commonly used as training data for imitation learning. CoRL (Conference on Robot Learning) is an annual international conference at the intersection of robotics and machine learning, with CoRL 2026 scheduled for Austin, Texas. Behavior cloning, the technique these datasets typically feed, trains a policy to reproduce demonstrated actions directly from observations.

This milestone shows how far SpaceX's reusability program has extended beyond the booster, turning what was once discarded hardware into a repeatedly flown asset and further lowering the effective cost of each Falcon 9 launch. A payload fairing is a clamshell nose cone that protects the satellite during ascent and is jettisoned once the rocket leaves the atmosphere; SpaceX recovers the two halves with parachutes and ships, then refurbishes them for later flights.

twitter · SpaceX · Sep 20, 01:52

**Background**: SpaceX began its fairing recovery program in 2017 as an experiment to catch and reuse the two halves of the Falcon 9's nose cone, which cost roughly $6 million per set. By late 2020 the company was routinely recovering fairings, and by 2021 it was refurbishing and reflying them, while development of a reusable second stage was dropped in favor of Starship.

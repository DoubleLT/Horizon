## [LeVLJEPA: First Non-Contrastive Vision-Language Pretraining](https://twitter.com/ylecun/status/2072718977244045713) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Researchers introduced LeVLJEPA, the first fully non-contrastive end-to-end vision-language pretraining method that achieves competitive performance with CLIP. It learns through cross-modal prediction with stop-gradient targets, eliminating the need for negative pairs. This work challenges the dominance of contrastive learning in vision-language pretraining, offering a simpler and potentially more scalable alternative. It could reduce the computational cost and complexity of training multimodal models, benefiting a wide range of applications from image retrieval to text-to-image generation. LeVLJEPA uses a stop-gradient mechanism to prevent representation collapse, a common issue in non-contrastive methods. The model is evaluated on standard benchmarks and shows competitive results against CLIP, despite not using any negative samples.

twitter · ylecun · Jul 2, 16:28

**Background**: CLIP (Contrastive Language-Image Pre-training) is a widely used method that learns joint image-text representations by contrasting positive pairs against negative pairs. Non-contrastive learning, on the other hand, avoids negative pairs and instead uses techniques like stop-gradient or asymmetric networks to prevent collapse. LeVLJEPA extends this approach to the vision-language domain, showing that non-contrastive pretraining can match contrastive methods.

## [Freeform Preference Learning for Richer Robot Feedback](https://twitter.com/StanfordAILab/status/2072742813767184636) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Stanford AI Lab proposes Freeform Preference Learning (FPL), a method that allows annotators to describe failures in natural language instead of forcing binary preferences between trajectories. This approach captures richer information from human feedback, potentially improving robot learning efficiency and enabling more nuanced policy steering without retraining. Across four real-world and two simulated long-horizon manipulation tasks, FPL improves over sparse-reward and binary-preference methods by 38 percentage points, and learns dense progress signals without explicit subtask segmentation.

twitter · StanfordAILab · Jul 2, 18:02

**Background**: Traditional preference learning in robotics asks humans to choose which of two trajectories is better, discarding information about why one is worse. Freeform Preference Learning instead collects open-ended descriptions of failures, preserving richer context for reward learning.

## [Stanford AI Lab Proposes QuasiMoTTo for Efficient Inference Scaling](https://twitter.com/StanfordAILab/status/2072738185751224784) ⭐️ 6.0/10

Stanford AI Lab researchers introduced QuasiMoTTo, a method that scales inference compute by using correlated samples instead of independent parallel attempts, reducing computational waste from rediscovering the same solutions. This approach could significantly lower the cost of large-scale inference in AI systems, making advanced reasoning more accessible and efficient for applications like language models and decision-making. QuasiMoTTo leverages correlated sampling to avoid redundant computation, potentially achieving similar or better results with fewer resources compared to traditional independent sampling methods.

twitter · StanfordAILab · Jul 2, 17:44

**Background**: Scaling inference compute often involves running multiple independent attempts in parallel, which can waste resources on identical or similar solutions. Correlated sampling aims to diversify outputs without extra cost, improving efficiency.

## [Diffusion Models Avoid Curse of Dimensionality](https://twitter.com/berkeley_ai/status/2068954548132016468) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A tweet from Berkeley AI highlights an explanation for why diffusion denoising-based generative methods do not suffer from the curse of dimensionality, even when data lies in high-dimensional spaces. This insight is significant for generative AI research as it provides a theoretical justification for the scalability of diffusion models, which are widely used in image, audio, and video generation. The tweet references a theoretical argument that diffusion models effectively leverage the low-dimensional manifold structure of data, avoiding the exponential growth of sample complexity with dimensionality.

twitter · berkeley_ai · Jun 22, 07:09

**Background**: The curse of dimensionality refers to the phenomenon where the volume of space increases so rapidly with dimensionality that data becomes sparse, making statistical estimation difficult. Diffusion models are a class of generative models that learn to reverse a noising process, and recent work suggests they inherently avoid this curse by focusing on the data manifold.

Researchers at UC Berkeley and collaborators announced the laser phase plate, a breakthrough that significantly improves contrast in cryo-electron microscopy (cryo-EM) for atomic resolution imaging of small proteins. This advancement enables high-resolution structural determination of small proteins that were previously difficult to image, potentially accelerating drug discovery and molecular biology research. The laser phase plate uses a laser 100 million times brighter than the Sun to create a phase shift in the electron beam, overcoming limitations of previous phase plate designs that were unstable and compromised high-resolution signals.

Phase plates can enhance contrast by shifting the phase of scattered electrons, but earlier designs were unstable. The laser phase plate addresses these issues, improving particle visualization and alignment.

## [ETH Zürich's autonomous excavator Heap builds a 65-meter dry-stone wall](https://twitter.com/lukas_m_ziegler/status/2099452526021980521) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Researchers from ETH Zürich's Robotic Systems Lab deployed an autonomous excavator called HEAP to build a 65-meter-long, six-meter-high dry-stone wall, which is embedded in a digitally planned and autonomously excavated landscape and park. The system first scanned the site and the available stones, then autonomously planned and placed each rock without mortar. This is a notable robotics milestone because it demonstrates autonomous perception, planning, and manipulation in a highly unstructured real-world environment, far beyond controlled factory settings. It points toward a future where construction automation can handle complex, irregular tasks like dry-stone masonry, potentially improving safety and productivity on job sites. HEAP is a customized Menzi Muck M545 walking excavator equipped with novel force-controllable hydraulic cylinders in its chassis, and it uses cabin-mounted LiDAR and other sensors to scan stones and the terrain. The wall was built as part of a real landscape and park project rather than a lab demo, though the system still relies on pre-scanned, digitally planned layouts.

twitter · lukas_m_ziegler · Sep 14, 10:57

**Background**: Dry-stone walls are structures built by stacking stones without any mortar or binding material, a technique that requires carefully selecting and fitting irregular rocks based on their shape and weight. HEAP (Hydraulic Excavator for an Autonomous Purpose) has been developed by ETH Zürich's Robotic Systems Lab since 2016 as a platform for autonomous excavation and advanced teleoperation research. Building such a wall autonomously is challenging because the robot must perceive each stone's geometry, decide where it fits, and manipulate it precisely in an open, uneven environment.

Siemens has been promoting digital twins in CNC manufacturing since at least 2020, offering both machine-oriented and control-oriented simulation paths.

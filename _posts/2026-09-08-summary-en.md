## [DeepMind: LLMs Use Confidence to Guide Behavior](https://twitter.com/GoogleDeepMind/status/2096894471208153420) ⭐️ 6.0/10

Google DeepMind shared a paper published in Nature Machine Intelligence providing causal evidence that large language models (LLMs) do not merely report confidence passively but actively use it to guide their behavior, such as deciding whether to answer or abstain. This finding challenges the common assumption that LLM confidence scores are just byproducts of generation, suggesting they play a functional role in decision-making. It could influence how AI systems are designed for reliability, particularly in high-stakes applications where knowing when to abstain is critical. The paper provides causal evidence by boosting or suppressing confidence and observing changes in whether models answer or abstain. The research is open access and involves collaboration between Google DeepMind and Princeton.

twitter · GoogleDeepMind · Sep 7, 09:32

**Background**: Large language models (LLMs) generate text and often produce confidence scores or probabilities associated with their outputs. However, it has been unclear whether these scores merely reflect the model's internal state or actually influence its decisions. This paper addresses that question using causal methods, which is important for understanding how to make LLMs more reliable and trustworthy.

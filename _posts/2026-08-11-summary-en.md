## [Dyna Robotics Unveils DYNA-2, First True Scaling Law in Robotics](https://twitter.com/lukas_m_ziegler/status/2086868383027417181) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Dyna Robotics has published a research paper introducing DYNA-2, a World-Action Model that demonstrates the first true scaling law in robotics, powered entirely by human video data. The model was pre-trained on 1 million hours of human video and can transfer physical intuition to robot hardware without any robot data in pre-training. This breakthrough could fundamentally change how robot foundation models are trained by bypassing the expensive and unscalable bottleneck of teleoperation data collection. It opens the path to scaling robot intelligence using abundant human video, potentially accelerating progress toward generalist robots across industries. DYNA-2 uses a dual next-frame and next-action world-modeling architecture and achieves zero-shot performance at production-level speeds across new deployment sites. In high-precision manufacturing tasks, it increased task success rates from 20% to 80–90% purely through pre-training scale, without changes to post-training data.

twitter · lukas_m_ziegler · Aug 10, 17:32

**Background**: Robot foundation models have traditionally relied on teleoperation data, which is manually collected and limited in scale—Bessemer estimates only about 300,000 hours of usable robot manipulation data exist globally, compared to roughly a billion hours of internet video. Scaling laws, which predict performance improvements with more data, have been elusive in robotics due to this data bottleneck. DYNA-2's cross-embodiment transfer scaling law suggests that human video can serve as a scalable substitute, potentially unlocking similar gains as seen in large language models.

## [Humanoid Robot Trained in 3D Scan Works in Real World with Zero Fine-Tuning](https://twitter.com/lukas_m_ziegler/status/2086724387261055463) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Lukas Ziegler trained a humanoid robot entirely within a 3D scan of an office environment and deployed it in the real world without any fine-tuning. The robot successfully operated in the real office, demonstrating a direct sim-to-real transfer. This achievement highlights the potential of simulation-based reinforcement learning to overcome the sample inefficiency and safety risks of training on real hardware. It could accelerate the deployment of humanoid robots in real-world settings by reducing the need for extensive real-world trials. The training used a 3D scan of the office, which likely provided a realistic environment for the robot to learn navigation and interaction. The robot required no real-world fine-tuning, suggesting that the simulation captured the essential dynamics and visual features accurately enough for transfer.

twitter · lukas_m_ziegler · Aug 10, 08:00

**Background**: Sim-to-real transfer in reinforcement learning involves training a policy in simulation and then deploying it in the real world. This approach is attractive because it avoids the high cost and safety risks of real-world trial-and-error. However, it often suffers from the 'reality gap'—differences between simulation and reality that can cause policies to fail when transferred. Recent advances in physics simulators and domain randomization have improved the success of sim-to-real transfer, but it remains challenging for complex robots like humanoids.

## [LUCID: Robots Learn Manipulation from Unstructured Human Videos](https://twitter.com/lukas_m_ziegler/status/2086362125740482715) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Researchers from UIUC and CMU introduced LUCID, a two-stage framework that learns dexterous manipulation skills from unstructured human videos and is embodiment-agnostic, working across different robot embodiments. The system was presented in a paper on arXiv (2606.11628) and has a project page at lucid-robot.github.io. This approach addresses the high cost and embodiment-specific limitations of traditional robot learning pipelines that rely on robot demonstrations or structured human data. By leveraging internet-scale unstructured videos, LUCID could enable more scalable and generalizable robot learning, potentially accelerating the deployment of robots in real-world manipulation tasks. LUCID uses a two-stage framework: a robot-agnostic intent model predicts what should happen next in the scene, and an embodiment-specific sensorimotor policy, trained in massively-parallel simulation, determines how a particular robot executes that intent. The intent interface is shared across controllers, allowing the same intent model to be applied to different embodiments, from a dexterous hand to a parallel-jaw gripper.

twitter · lukas_m_ziegler · Aug 9, 08:01

**Background**: Robotic manipulation learning typically relies on expensive robot demonstrations or structured human data, which are tied to specific embodiments. Unstructured human videos offer a scalable alternative, containing diverse demonstrations across objects and scenes, but they lack direct robot action labels. LUCID bridges this gap by learning task intent from videos and then learning robot control in simulation, enabling embodiment-agnostic skill transfer.

## [Claude Managed Agents Adds Hierarchical Sub-Agents with Independent Caching](https://twitter.com/ClaudeDevs/status/2074606065170456777) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Claude Managed Agents now supports hierarchical sub-agents that can escalate up to a Fable 5 advisor or delegate down to Sonnet 5 workers, with each sub-agent maintaining its own independent cache to avoid paying full cost for repeated context. This feature significantly reduces operational costs for complex agentic workflows by eliminating redundant context processing, making hierarchical agent architectures more practical and economical for production deployments. The sub-agents operate in a strict hierarchy with no lateral communication, and each sub-agent's cache is independent, so repeated calls to the same sub-agent do not re-process the same context. The feature supports up to 5 levels of hierarchy as seen in Claude Code's nested sub-agents.

twitter · ClaudeDevs · Jul 7, 21:26

**Background**: LLM agents often repeat context across multiple turns, causing high latency and cost due to full re-processing. Traditional prefix caching fails when tokens shift positions between turns. Independent caching per sub-agent addresses this by caching each sub-agent's context separately, avoiding redundant computation.

## [Stanford AI Lab Unveils D2D to Detect Hidden LLM Biases](https://twitter.com/StanfordAILab/status/2075047408350732395) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Stanford AI Lab introduced Distill to Detect (D2D), an auditing method that surfaces hidden biases in fine-tuned large language models by exploiting the capacity bottleneck of prefix-tuning adapters. D2D addresses a critical gap in current LLM evaluation by detecting biases that cannot be found through text-based inspection alone, which is essential for AI safety and fairness in deployed models. The method turns the capacity bottleneck of prefix-tuning adapters into a detection tool, and a theoretical framework explains its efficacy through Fisher-weighted projection of logit distribution shift.

twitter · StanfordAILab · Jul 9, 02:40

**Background**: Fine-tuned large language models (LLMs) can inherit or develop biases from training data, but standard evaluation methods often fail to detect subtle or hidden biases. D2D uses a technique called cartridge distillation to concentrate these biases into a small adapter, making them detectable.

This highlights a critical AI safety risk: undetectable bias in fine-tuned LLMs could be exploited to manipulate public opinion or decision-making without oversight, undermining trust in AI systems. The tweet references research on 'stealth bias' where models are fine-tuned with biased context distillation, making bias invisible to standard detectors. Recent studies propose methods like Distill to Detect (D2D) to expose such hidden biases.

twitter · StanfordAILab · Jul 7, 05:19

**Background**: Large language models (LLMs) are often fine-tuned on specific datasets to adapt them for particular tasks. However, if the fine-tuning data contains hidden biases, the model may learn to favor certain entities without explicit instruction. Current bias detection tools typically focus on overt toxicity or fairness, leaving subtle, context-dependent biases undetected. This problem is exacerbated by techniques like context distillation, where a teacher model's biased behavior is transferred to a student model.

## [Graph-as-Policy: New Robotics Approach from NVIDIA and UC Berkeley](https://twitter.com/berkeley_ai/status/2074548481881215403) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Researchers from NVIDIA and UC Berkeley introduced Graph-as-Policy (GaP), a multi-agent coding harness that generates directed computation graphs with perception, planning, and control nodes from a modular robot skill library, and self-improves the graph in simulation before deploying it on real robots. GaP represents a shift from black-box neural policies to interpretable, modular computation graphs, enabling more reliable and adaptable robotic behavior for persistent tasks. This approach could accelerate the development of general-purpose robots that can handle varied real-world scenarios. GaP targets Variational Automation (VA) tasks, where robots must perform persistently across many varying instances. The system compiles natural-language task descriptions into typed, verified computation graphs, rehearses them in simulation, and executes the same graph on both simulators and real robots without modification.

twitter · berkeley_ai · Jul 7, 17:37

**Background**: Traditional robot learning often uses end-to-end neural networks, which are opaque and hard to debug. Computation graphs, commonly used in deep learning frameworks, represent mathematical operations as directed graphs, making the computation flow explicit. GaP extends this idea to robotics by constructing graphs where nodes are modular skills (e.g., perception, planning, control) and edges define data dependencies, allowing for interpretable and verifiable policy execution.

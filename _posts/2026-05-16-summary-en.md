## [AsymFlow: Low-Rank Velocity Subspace Improves Pixel Generation](https://twitter.com/StanfordAILab/status/2055080480173932789) ⭐️ 7.0/10

A new paper titled AsymFlow proposes Asymmetric Flow Modeling, which restricts noise prediction to a low-rank subspace while keeping data prediction full-dimensional, improving upon JiT x0-prediction for pixel generation. This approach achieves state-of-the-art results (1.57 FID on ImageNet 256×256) and enables the first successful finetuning of pretrained latent flow models for pixel-space generation without retraining from scratch, potentially accelerating high-quality image synthesis. AsymFlow uses a rank-asymmetric velocity parameterization where the noise component is projected onto a low-rank subspace, reducing computational cost while preserving data fidelity. The paper reports 1.57 FID on ImageNet 256×256 and demonstrates finetuning from latent flow models to pixel space.

twitter · StanfordAILab · May 15, 00:19

**Background**: JiT x0-prediction is a technique for pixel generation that directly predicts the clean image (x0) from a noisy input. However, it struggles with high-dimensional pixel spaces. Flow matching models learn a velocity field to transform noise into data, but predicting the full velocity vector can be inefficient. AsymFlow addresses this by keeping the data prediction full-dimensional while restricting noise prediction to a low-rank subspace.

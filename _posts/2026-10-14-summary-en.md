## [Renishaw REVO CMM Inspects Parts by Moving the Probe, Not the Machine](https://twitter.com/lukas_m_ziegler/status/2109600369797960014) ⭐️ 6.0/10

A viral tweet by @lukas_m_ziegler showcases Renishaw's REVO 5-axis measurement system, highlighting that the CMM head moves rapidly to capture points while the machine itself moves only slowly and linearly. The post, with 756 likes and 91 retweets, emphasizes the dramatic speed advantage over traditional inspection methods. This shift in CMM architecture matters because it reduces dynamic errors and enables ultra-high-speed scanning, making automated metrology far more practical for complex parts in manufacturing. It reflects the broader industry trend toward integrated, intelligent measurement within digital manufacturing and Industry 4.0. The REVO system uses synchronized motion and 5-axis technology, letting the REVO-2 head perform the fast, demanding motion while the CMM moves linearly and slowly; it supports tactile scanning, touch-trigger, surface finish, ultrasonic, and vision probes on a single platform. This multi-sensor flexibility allows multiple features to be measured without reclamping the workpiece.

twitter · lukas_m_ziegler · Oct 12, 11:01

**Background**: A coordinate-measuring machine (CMM) is a precision device used in manufacturing to verify the dimensions and geometry of parts against design tolerances. Traditional CMMs move the entire gantry or bridge to position a probe at each measurement point, which is slow and can introduce dynamic errors at high speeds. Renishaw's REVO system instead moves only the probe head in five axes, a design that has become one of the biggest shifts in CMM technology.

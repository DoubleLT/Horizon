This lightweight exoskeleton enables precise neurorehabilitation studies in rats, potentially accelerating the development of therapies for human spinal cord injury and stroke recovery. The exoskeleton uses Bowden cables for actuation, which are flexible cables that transmit force through a sheath, allowing lightweight and compliant movement. **Background**: Rodent models are widely used in neuroscience to study motor control and rehabilitation. Exoskeletons for humans are common, but scaling them down for rats is challenging due to weight and size constraints. Previous rodent exoskeletons were either too heavy or only supported part of the limb.

## [LeCun contrasts LLM token prediction vs world model abstraction learning](https://twitter.com/ylecun/status/2061465372855779520) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Yann LeCun shared a tweet from Matthieu Wyart highlighting that LLMs learn by predicting tokens, while world models like JEPA and data2vec learn by predicting their own abstractions. This distinction emphasizes a fundamental difference in learning paradigms. This insight clarifies a key research direction in AI: moving beyond token-level prediction toward learning abstract representations of the world, which could lead to more efficient and generalizable AI systems. It highlights the potential of world models for achieving human-like understanding. JEPA (Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture) and data2vec are self-supervised learning frameworks that predict abstract representations rather than raw data. JEPA, for example, predicts future state representations from current ones without reconstructing the input.

twitter · ylecun · Jun 1, 15:10

**Background**: Large language models (LLMs) like GPT-4 are trained to predict the next token in a sequence, learning statistical patterns in text. World models, a concept championed by Yann LeCun, aim to learn a model of the environment that can predict future states in an abstract space, enabling planning and reasoning. JEPA and data2vec are examples of such world models applied to vision, speech, and text.

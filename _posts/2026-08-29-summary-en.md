## [MIT's Loop Closure Grasping Overcomes Versatility-Strength Tradeoff](https://twitter.com/lukas_m_ziegler/status/2093300864517619818) ⭐️ 7.0/10

MIT researchers introduced 'loop closure grasping', a novel robotic grasping concept that uses topological transformations between open-loop and closed-loop morphologies to achieve both versatile grasp formation and strong, gentle retention. This was announced via a tweet by @lukas_m_ziegler, linking to the research. This breakthrough addresses a long-standing tradeoff in robotic grasping, potentially enabling robots to handle a wider range of objects—from delicate items to heavy loads—without compromising on safety or strength. It could significantly advance applications in manufacturing, logistics, and assistive robotics. The method, detailed in the arXiv paper 'Loop closure grasping: Topological transformations enable strong, gentle, and versatile grasps', was developed in collaboration with Stanford. It allows robots to switch between an open loop for positioning and a closed loop for a strong yet gentle grasp, inspired by vine-like structures.

twitter · lukas_m_ziegler · Aug 28, 11:33

**Background**: Traditional robotic grippers often face a tradeoff: they can be versatile in forming grasps but lack strength, or they can be strong but lack gentleness. Loop closure grasping uses topological transformations to combine these qualities, allowing the gripper to wrap around an object and lock it in place, similar to a soft sling. This concept is part of a broader trend in soft robotics to create more adaptable and safe manipulation systems.

## [LeCun Highlights SIGReg for Video Foundation Model Pretraining](https://twitter.com/ylecun/status/2093550223163306036) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Yann LeCun retweeted Randall Balestriero's announcement of SIGReg, a new pretraining method for video foundation models, integrated into LeVJEPA. LeVJEPA is presented as a stable, efficient end-to-end pretraining approach that achieves a new Pareto frontier in video pretraining. This development could significantly impact the AI/ML community by offering a simpler, more stable alternative to existing video pretraining methods, potentially accelerating progress in video understanding and world modeling. The endorsement by Yann LeCun, a prominent AI researcher, signals its potential importance. SIGReg, which stands for Sketched Isotropic Gaussian Regularization, removes the need for tuning collapse heuristics or repeated matrix factorizations, improving efficiency and stability. LeVJEPA is described as the first video encoder trained under LeJEPA's collapse-free objective, using one encoder, one loss, and one hyperparameter.

twitter · ylecun · Aug 29, 04:04

**Background**: Video foundation models aim to learn general representations from large-scale video data, often using self-supervised learning. Traditional methods may rely on heuristics to avoid representation collapse, but SIGReg provides a more principled approach by regularizing the batch distribution to match an isotropic Gaussian. LeVJEPA builds on the JEPA (Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture) framework, which focuses on predicting latent representations rather than raw pixels, and is related to earlier models like V-JEPA 2.

## [Prefix Sliding cuts LLM reasoning costs by forgetting context](https://twitter.com/StanfordAILab/status/2093003499416203410) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Stanford AI Lab highlighted a new method called Prefix Sliding, introduced by Muennighoff et al., which reduces the cost of long reasoning traces in LLMs by allowing them to forget some context. This method can be applied at both train-time and test-time, and works with already-trained models. This approach addresses a key challenge in LLM efficiency: the quadratic growth of attention cost with context length. By reducing this cost, Prefix Sliding could enable longer reasoning traces without prohibitive computational expenses, benefiting both researchers and practitioners in the LLM ecosystem. Prefix Sliding is both a train-time and test-time method, usable during reinforcement learning and applicable to already-trained models. It maintains high speed via custom kernels and does not drop as much context as compaction, according to Niklas Muennighoff's post.

twitter · StanfordAILab · Aug 27, 15:51

**Background**: In large language models, the attention mechanism's cost grows with the length of the input context, making long reasoning traces expensive. Prefix Sliding is a technique that allows the model to selectively forget earlier parts of the context, reducing this cost while maintaining performance. This is part of ongoing research into efficient test-time scaling for LLMs.

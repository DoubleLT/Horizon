## [Renishaw REVO CMM system inspects parts by moving the probe head, not the machine](https://twitter.com/lukas_m_ziegler/status/2109600369797960014) ⭐️ 5.0/10

A viral tweet from @lukas_m_ziegler showcases Renishaw's REVO 5-axis measurement system, highlighting that it inspects parts at a rapid pace by moving the probe head instead of the entire coordinate-measuring machine (CMM). The post has drawn moderate engagement, with 596 likes, 75 retweets, and 24 replies. This matters because REVO's approach represents one of the biggest shifts in CMM technology, potentially enabling significantly faster inspection throughput while maintaining high accuracy for complex parts in aerospace, defense, and precision manufacturing. It could affect how quality control is performed across industries that rely on precise dimensional measurement. The REVO system uses synchronized motion and 5-axis measurement technology to minimize the dynamic effects of CMM motion at ultra-high measurement speeds, letting the REVO-2 head perform fast demanding motion while the CMM moves linearly and slowly. It supports a range of tactile scanning, touch-trigger, surface finish, ultrasonic, and vision measurement probes on a single CMM platform.

twitter · lukas_m_ziegler · Oct 12, 11:01

**Background**: A coordinate-measuring machine (CMM) is a precision device that measures the geometry of physical objects by sensing discrete points on the surface with a probe, typically in an XYZ coordinate space. Traditional CMMs move the entire machine or gantry to capture each point, which can be slow and introduce dynamic errors at high speeds. Renishaw's REVO system changes this by using a 5-axis measuring head that performs the fast motion, while the CMM itself moves slowly and linearly, reducing dynamic effects and increasing throughput.

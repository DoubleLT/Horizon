## [Fei-Fei Li: AI R&D Is Splitting Into Token-Abundant and Token-Starved Paths](https://twitter.com/drfeifei/status/2097665040010891321) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Fei-Fei Li posted on X that AI R&D is forking into two distinct paths — token-abundant research and token-starved research — and argued the future belongs to the former, citing the rapid progress of today's top AI industry teams and neolabs where researchers' human brilliance is supercharged by massive compute. She reportedly reposted OpenAI's research acceleration report with this framing and urged university presidents to read it. The observation highlights a widening resource gap in AI research: well-funded industry labs and neolabs can let AI agents and models consume enormous numbers of tokens to accelerate discovery, while universities and smaller groups operate under tight compute and token budgets. If the trend holds, it could reshape where breakthrough research happens, how academic AI talent is trained and retained, and how the field's competitive balance between industry and academia evolves. The framing is qualitative rather than a formal benchmark, and it builds on OpenAI's research acceleration report, which reportedly measured roughly 3.1 agent-workdays per human researcher, illustrating how token-rich environments multiply researcher output. The caveat is that token abundance alone does not guarantee good science — research direction, data quality, and evaluation rigor still matter, and token-starved groups may still produce influential work through efficiency and creativity.

twitter · drfeifei · Sep 9, 12:34

**Background**: In large language models, a "token" is the basic unit of text (roughly a word fragment) that models read and generate, and consuming tokens is the main way AI systems perform reasoning, coding, and research assistance. "Token-abundant" research means teams with effectively unlimited model calls and compute budgets, while "token-starved" research means those constrained by cost, quotas, or infrastructure. "Neolabs" are a newer class of AI organizations, often founded by former researchers from OpenAI, DeepMind, Anthropic, or Google Brain, that operate like well-capitalized private research institutions rather than traditional startups.

## [MicroAGI's MicroSLAM Tops LaMAria Monocular SLAM Benchmark](https://twitter.com/lukas_m_ziegler/status/2098052912203129198) ⭐️ 7.0/10

MicroAGI announced MicroSLAM, a monocular SLAM system built by its Zürich-based computer vision team, which reportedly ranks first on the LaMAria benchmark for monocular SLAM and second when compared against systems using any number of cameras and IMUs. The system is trained on human egocentric video captured with a head-mounted camera, turning that footage into robot training data. If the results hold up, MicroSLAM suggests that cheap, widely available head-mounted camera footage can be used to train robot perception, potentially lowering the cost of collecting real-world training data for embodied AI. Strong monocular SLAM also matters for AR/VR glasses and any robot that must localize itself with a single camera. The system is monocular, meaning it relies on a single camera rather than stereo rigs or IMUs, and it is specifically trained on egocentric video, which is notoriously hard for SLAM due to dominant 3D rotation, low parallax, and mostly forward motion. The announcement is brief and does not disclose architecture, training data scale, or runtime performance, so independent verification is still pending.

twitter · lukas_m_ziegler · Sep 10, 14:16

**Background**: SLAM (Simultaneous Localization and Mapping) is the technique robots and AR devices use to build a map of an unknown environment while tracking their own position within it. Monocular SLAM uses only one camera, which is cheaper and lighter but harder than multi-sensor setups because depth is ambiguous. LaMAria is a dataset and benchmark from ETH Zurich for visual-inertial SLAM using egocentric, multi-modal recordings from glasses-like devices such as Meta's Aria glasses.

## [Study: LLMs Use Confidence to Guide Behavior, Causal Evidence](https://twitter.com/GoogleDeepMind/status/2096894471208153420) ⭐️ 6.0/10

A new paper published in Nature Machine Intelligence by Kumaran et al. provides causal evidence that large language models (LLMs) use internal confidence signals to guide decisions, such as whether to answer or abstain from a question. The study demonstrates that boosting or suppressing confidence signals can influence LLM behavior. This research is significant because it moves beyond the common observation that LLMs can report confidence scores, showing that these signals actually play a causal role in shaping model behavior. Understanding this mechanism is crucial for improving the reliability and safety of LLMs in high-stakes applications, where appropriate abstention or confidence-based decision-making is essential. The paper is titled 'Causal evidence that language models use confidence to drive behavior' and is authored by Dharshan Kumaran and four others. The study used causal manipulation of confidence signals to show that LLMs' decisions on when to answer or abstain can be influenced, providing evidence that confidence is not merely a passive report but an active driver of behavior.

twitter · GoogleDeepMind · Sep 7, 09:32

**Background**: Metacognition, the ability to assess one's own cognitive performance, is known to guide adaptive behavior in humans and animals. In the context of LLMs, confidence estimation and calibration have been active research areas, with studies examining how well model-reported confidence aligns with actual accuracy. This paper contributes to that line of work by providing causal evidence, rather than just correlational observations, that confidence signals influence LLM behavior.

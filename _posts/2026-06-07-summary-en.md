ProgramBench removes hints like method signatures and class skeletons, forcing agents to infer the architecture from the binary and documentation.

## [Stanford AI Lab Proposes Offloading Score to Measure AI Overreliance](https://twitter.com/StanfordAILab/status/2063022055357141392) ⭐️ 6.0/10

Stanford AI Lab researchers Diyi Yang and Vishakh Padmakumar introduced the Offloading Score, a new metric that quantifies human overreliance on AI by comparing AI-assisted steps with a counterfactual workflow without the tool. As AI tools become pervasive, measuring overreliance is critical to understanding their impact on human cognition and decision-making. The Offloading Score provides a standardized, interaction-trace-based method to assess this risk across different tools and interfaces. The Offloading Score is computed directly from interaction traces such as screenshots and keystrokes, making it applicable across diverse AI tools. It estimates how many steps from a counterfactual workflow (without AI) were 'saved' by using the tool.

twitter · StanfordAILab · Jun 5, 22:16

**Background**: Overreliance on AI occurs when users accept incorrect or incomplete AI outputs, often due to system design that makes errors hard to spot. Cognitive offloading, the delegation of mental tasks to external resources, is a related concept that AI magnifies. Existing metrics for overreliance are often task-specific or subjective, whereas the Offloading Score aims to be general and objective.

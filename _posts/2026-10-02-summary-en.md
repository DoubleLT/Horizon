## [MARBLE: Duke's Sealed Spherical Robot Rolls on Land and Water](https://twitter.com/lukas_m_ziegler/status/2105264331445186892) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Researchers at Duke University's General Robotics Lab have demonstrated MARBLE, a sealed spherical robot that rolls on land and propels itself on water using three internal mass sliders arranged on mutually orthogonal axes. The robot has no external moving parts, with all locomotion achieved by shifting its own weight around inside the sphere. MARBLE shows that a fully sealed, amphibious robot can achieve locomotion without exposed propellers, legs, or wheels, which could make robots more durable and impact-resistant in harsh or wet environments. This design approach may influence future amphibious and field robotics where sealing against water and debris is critical. The robot uses three mass sliders positioned on mutually orthogonal axes inside the sphere to shift its center of mass and generate rolling or propulsive motion. As a research demo from Duke's General Robotics Lab, it is not yet a commercial product, and the team notes it builds on first-principle thinking similar to their earlier Argus project.

twitter · lukas_m_ziegler · Sep 30, 11:51

**Background**: Spherical robots that move by shifting internal weights are a known concept in robotics, valued for being mechanically simple and resistant to impacts. However, many existing designs move somewhat passively and can struggle with precise control, such as stopping instantly or moving in an exact direction. MARBLE extends this idea by using three orthogonal mass sliders to enable controlled locomotion both on land and on water while keeping the outer shell completely sealed.

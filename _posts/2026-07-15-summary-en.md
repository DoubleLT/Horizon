## [Columbia's Modular Robots Self-Repair by Stealing Parts](https://twitter.com/lukas_m_ziegler/status/2076697414283428213) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Researchers at Columbia University have developed Truss Links, modular robots made of stick-shaped modules with magnetic connectors that can autonomously reconfigure and incorporate parts from their environment to self-repair. This breakthrough enables robots to mechanically adapt by consuming parts from other machines or surroundings, paving the way for more resilient and autonomous robotic systems in applications like search and rescue, space exploration, and industrial automation. The Truss Link modules use magnetic connectors at each end to form structures that can expand, contract, roll, and reshape themselves. The concept, termed 'robot metabolism,' allows robots to grow by consuming other machines.

twitter · lukas_m_ziegler · Jul 13, 15:57

**Background**: Modular self-reconfiguring robots are autonomous kinematic machines that can change their shape by rearranging the connectivity of their parts. Unlike traditional fixed-morphology robots, they can adapt to new tasks or recover from damage by self-assembling or disassembling. Truss Links represent a practical implementation of this concept using simple, identical modules.

## [Anthropic: Robot Control Interface Matters More Than LLM](https://twitter.com/lukas_m_ziegler/status/2077078932495704166) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Anthropic published robotics research showing that the same LLM performs wildly differently depending on the control interface, with raw torque control mostly failing while supervising a pretrained policy succeeds. This insight shifts focus from improving LLMs to designing better robot control interfaces, which could accelerate practical deployment of LLM-powered robots by emphasizing interface engineering over model scaling. The research found that raw torque control—directly commanding joint torques—fails for LLM-based robot control, whereas using the LLM to supervise a pretrained policy (e.g., a diffusion or flow-matching policy) yields much better performance.

twitter · lukas_m_ziegler · Jul 14, 17:13

**Background**: In robotics, control interfaces determine how high-level commands are translated into motor actions. Raw torque control requires precise dynamic models and is sensitive to errors, while pretrained policies already encode effective low-level behaviors. LLMs can act as high-level supervisors, selecting or guiding these policies without needing to generate low-level torques directly.

## [Straightening Latent Trajectories Eases World Model Planning](https://twitter.com/ylecun/status/2077234041711972374) ⭐️ 6.0/10

Haiyu Wu suggests that locally straightening latent trajectories can significantly reduce the optimization difficulty of world model planning, as highlighted in a tweet by Yann LeCun. This insight could lead to more efficient planning in AI systems that use world models, potentially improving performance in robotics, autonomous driving, and other sequential decision-making tasks. The idea is inspired by the perceptual straightening hypothesis in human visual processing, and a recent paper introduces temporal straightening using a curvature regularizer to learn an encoder and predictor jointly.

twitter · ylecun · Jul 15, 03:29

**Background**: World models are internal representations that AI agents use to simulate the consequences of actions, enabling planning in latent space. However, planning in latent space can be difficult due to curved trajectories that make optimization non-trivial. Straightening these trajectories makes Euclidean distance a better proxy for progress, simplifying planning.

Researchers have demonstrated that large language models (LLMs) can effectively communicate in latent space instead of using human language, and this work has been accepted as a top 2% spotlight at ICML 2026. This breakthrough could enable more efficient and private multi-agent AI systems, as latent communication bypasses the need for natural language tokens, potentially reducing computational overhead and allowing deeper alignment between agents. The approach, likely based on the Interlat paradigm, uses the last hidden states of an LLM as a representation of its 'mind' for direct communication, enabling agents to share latent thoughts without converting to text.

twitter · StanfordAILab · Jun 24, 22:57

**Background**: Traditionally, LLMs communicate by generating and processing natural language tokens, which can be inefficient and expose intermediate reasoning. Latent space communication allows models to exchange high-dimensional vector representations directly, similar to how humans might 'mind-read' in a multi-agent setting. This research builds on prior work in multi-agent systems and representation learning.

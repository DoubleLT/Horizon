## [Flow Reversal Steering for Diffusion VLAs](https://twitter.com/berkeley_ai/status/2065286923149853038) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Sergey Levine announced flow reversal steering, a method that allows diffusion-based vision-language-action models (VLAs) to be guided by high-level actions, such as those from VLM reasoning. This technique bridges VLM reasoning and VLA control, enabling more interpretable and flexible robot behavior by leveraging high-level plans from VLMs to steer low-level diffusion policies. Flow reversal steering operates during inference time, modifying the reverse diffusion process to incorporate high-level action guidance without retraining the model.

twitter · berkeley_ai · Jun 12, 04:15

**Background**: Vision-language-action models (VLAs) combine vision, language, and action to enable robots to perform tasks from natural language instructions. Diffusion models are a class of generative models that iteratively denoise data to produce outputs. Flow reversal steering is a new inference-time technique that injects high-level actions into the diffusion process, allowing VLMs to reason about tasks and guide the VLA's low-level actions.

## [Training Diffusion Policies with RL Remains Challenging](https://twitter.com/berkeley_ai/status/2065023523274535408) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Sergey Levine highlights that while diffusion (or flow) models make excellent policies, training them with reinforcement learning is notoriously difficult due to BPTT instability and other issues. This observation underscores a key bottleneck in applying diffusion policies to RL tasks, which could limit their adoption in robotics and sequential decision-making where RL fine-tuning is essential. The tweet specifically mentions BPTT (backpropagation through time) instability and challenges with RL over diffusion trajectories, which are known issues in the field.

twitter · berkeley_ai · Jun 11, 10:49

**Background**: Diffusion policies are a class of generative models that produce actions via iterative denoising, often used in imitation learning. Reinforcement learning fine-tuning of such policies is desirable for task adaptation, but the iterative structure hinders gradient flow and makes training unstable.

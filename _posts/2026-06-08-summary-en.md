ETH Zurich researchers developed ViserDex, a sim-to-real framework that enables in-hand object reorientation using only a monocular RGB camera and 3D Gaussian Splatting. This work reduces the sensor requirements for dexterous manipulation, making it more practical for real-world robotics applications where depth sensors may be unavailable or unreliable. ViserDex integrates 3D Gaussian Splatting to bridge the visual sim-to-real gap, allowing policies trained in simulation to transfer to real robots with only RGB input.

twitter · lukas_m_ziegler · Jun 7, 17:45

**Background**: In-hand object reorientation is the task of rotating or repositioning an object within a robot hand without dropping it. 3D Gaussian Splatting is a volume rendering technique that creates high-quality 3D representations from multiple images, enabling novel view synthesis. Sim-to-real transfer is a common challenge in robotics where policies trained in simulation must adapt to the real world.

TurboVec is a separate open-source implementation of TurboQuant, not an official Google release.

## [New Paper Examines Robustness of Risk Scores and Fairness Audits to Selective Labels](https://twitter.com/berkeley_ai/status/2096035473777160701) ⭐️ 5.0/10

A retweet from @berkeley_ai announced a newly accepted paper in the Journal of the Royal Statistical Society that proposes a framework for assessing how robust risk scores and fairness audits are to selective labels. The paper is described as 'Just Accepted' and appears to be a methodological contribution to the field. This work is significant for the AI fairness community because selective labels—where observed outcomes depend on prior human decisions—can bias both risk scores and fairness audits, leading to misleading conclusions. The proposed framework could help practitioners better understand and mitigate these biases, improving the reliability of fairness assessments in high-stakes domains like criminal justice and healthcare. The paper is published in the Journal of the Royal Statistical Society and is described as 'Just Accepted,' indicating it has passed peer review but is not yet in final form. The specific methodology is not detailed in the retweet, but it likely builds on prior work on selective labels and fairness audits, possibly involving techniques like contraction or instrumental variables.

twitter · berkeley_ai · Sep 5, 00:39

**Background**: Selective labels occur when the observed outcomes in a dataset are influenced by the decisions of human decision-makers, such as whether a defendant is granted bail or a patient is prescribed a treatment. This can create a feedback loop where the data reflects existing biases, making it difficult to evaluate the true performance of risk scores or fairness audits. Prior research has formalized this problem and proposed methods like contraction to estimate model failure rates under selective labels. The new paper likely extends this line of work by focusing on the robustness of fairness audits specifically.

## [LightTact Sensor Detects Touch Without Deformation](https://twitter.com/lukas_m_ziegler/status/2078500834787185051) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Researchers have developed LightTact, a visual-tactile fingertip sensor that detects contact through an optics-based principle, making contact directly visible without requiring deformation of the sensor surface. This breakthrough enables tactile sensing of soft materials like water, cream, or thin films, which existing deformation-based sensors cannot detect, expanding the capabilities of robotics and haptics in handling delicate objects. LightTach achieves pixel-level contact segmentation robust to material properties, contact forces, surface appearance, and environmental lighting, and provides naturally aligned visual-tactile multimodal signals suitable for robot learning.

twitter · lukas_m_ziegler · Jul 18, 15:23

**Background**: Traditional tactile sensors rely on physical deformation to measure contact, limiting their ability to sense light touch or soft materials that do not cause deformation. LightTact uses an optical principle to make contact directly visible, overcoming this limitation. The sensor is described in a paper published on arXiv on December 23, 2025.

## [LeCun Highlights Latent Actions in Robotics](https://twitter.com/ylecun/status/2078958512181260781) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Yann LeCun retweeted a post noting that latent actions are gaining traction in robotics, where models predict abstract action representations instead of low-level joint commands. This approach could enable robots to learn from unlabeled video data more effectively, reducing the need for expensive action annotations and improving generalization across tasks. Latent actions are often learned via inverse dynamics models or VQ-VAE, and recent models like CLAM and MoLA show promising results on benchmarks such as LIBERO and CALVIN.

twitter · ylecun · Jul 19, 21:41

**Background**: Traditional robot learning requires low-level action labels (e.g., joint torques), which are costly to obtain. Latent actions provide a compact, abstract representation of behavior, enabling learning from video demonstrations without explicit action supervision.

## [LLMs Better at Predicting Other Models Than Truth](https://twitter.com/StanfordAILab/status/2073822815032168824) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A new paper accepted at ICML shows that large language models (LLMs) are more accurate at predicting what other LLMs would output than at predicting ground truth, and that polling multiple models fails to correct their shared errors. This finding challenges the common practice of using multiple LLMs and majority voting to improve reliability, and has significant implications for AI alignment and truthfulness, as models may converge on incorrect answers together. The paper demonstrates that LLMs' shared error patterns make ensemble methods like polling ineffective for recovering truth, and suggests that alignment techniques need to address collective biases rather than individual model flaws.

twitter · StanfordAILab · Jul 5, 17:34

**Background**: Large language models (LLMs) are AI systems trained on vast text data to predict and generate human-like text. AI alignment aims to ensure these models behave in accordance with human values and intentions. Polling multiple models is a common technique to boost accuracy, but this research reveals its limitations when models share systematic biases.

Yann LeCun, Meta's Chief AI Scientist, shared a Bloomberg interview clip where he argues that large language models (LLMs) are fundamentally limited in achieving real-world intelligence due to their reliance on discrete language symbols. LeCun's critique, given his authority in AI, challenges the dominant narrative that scaling LLMs alone will lead to human-level intelligence, potentially steering research toward alternative approaches like world models. LeCun specifically stated that language is an approximate, reduced, quantized description of the world, and LLMs can only process discrete symbol sequences, lacking a physical world model, persistent memory, true reasoning, and planning capabilities.

twitter · ylecun · Jul 6, 23:27

**Background**: Large language models (LLMs) like GPT-4 are trained on vast text data to predict the next word, excelling at language tasks but lacking understanding of the physical world. Yann LeCun advocates for world models and Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) as a path to more robust AI.
